Section 01 — Overview

What this system does

When a prospect submits an enquiry form for any of MSS's three brands, someone on the team needs to act on it. That means understanding who they are, whether they're a real fit, and how to open the conversation well. That work takes time — and it has to happen for every lead, every day, across three brands.

This system handles the preparation. By the time the sales team opens a Telegram notification, the lead has already been validated, researched across the web, and scored against a brand-specific 100-point Ideal Customer Profile. The research is done. The team focuses entirely on the conversation and the decision — not the legwork before it.

Core principle Every decision — who to call, how to pitch, whether to follow up — stays with the team. The system handles the research and preparation so those decisions can be made faster, with better information.

Section 03 — How it works

What happens after someone submits a form

The pipeline runs automatically — no one needs to trigger it. Here is what happens in the roughly three minutes between a form submission and the Telegram notification.

0

Source detection & field normalisation

The system identifies which brand the lead came from by reading the form fields — Trakop leads have delivery volume fields, DairyTech leads have a dairy domain selector, MSS leads have a general message. Field names from different forms are mapped to a common format before anything else runs.

1

Validation — four checks before anything is saved

Missing or invalid fields → flagged as incomplete. Gibberish names, disposable emails, fake phone numbers → flagged as spam. Email already in the database → flagged as duplicate (the original lead's full report is included in the notification). Email domain matching a known competitor → flagged and stopped. Nothing is saved to the database until all four checks pass.

2

ICP Scoring — form data only, no searches yet

The lead is scored 0–100 against the Ideal Customer Profile for its brand. Five dimensions: business fit, operational scale, pain points, decision maker quality, and geography. Leads scoring below 30 (COLD) are rejected immediately. Everything else proceeds to research.

3

Web research — up to 7 targeted searches

The AI agent runs up to seven searches tailored to the brand and company. It looks for the website, LinkedIn, key contacts, company size, recent news, and — always — a dedicated revenue search. Every claim in the report must be cited with a source URL. No made-up data, no training knowledge used as fact.

3b

Cross-validation — score revisited against findings

The preliminary score is revised up or down based on what research actually found. If the business type doesn't match what the form said, or the company scale is very different from what was claimed, the score adjusts accordingly. If the revised score drops below 30, the lead is rejected at this stage — but the full research is included in the rejection notification so the team can see what was found.

4

Deliver — full briefing sent to Telegram

The complete lead report is sent to the sales team channel. It includes the ICP score with full reasoning, company research with source links, key contacts, a revenue estimate, and brand-specific sales enablement — a personalised icebreaker, a hypothesised pain point, and a pitch angle. The team has everything they need to open a strong first conversation.

Company name inference If a lead doesn't provide a company name but uses a business email domain, the system infers the company name from the domain — e.g. info@freshmill.co.ke becomes Fresh Mill. Personal email with no company → treated as an individual operator (B2C).
Section 04 — ICP Scoring

How leads are scored

Every lead is rated across five dimensions — business fit, scale, pain points, decision maker quality, and geography — using a scoring model specific to each brand. Scores total up to 110 raw points, then normalise to a clean 0–100 scale. Each lead lands in one of four tiers.

80–100 HOT Strong fit, clear need. Prioritise immediately.
50–79 WARM Decent fit, some gaps. Worth reaching out.
30–49 COOL Weak signals. Research runs before any decision is made.
0–29 COLD Poor fit. Rejected before research. Team is still notified.

The score is calculated twice — first from form data alone (Stage 2), then revised after web research (Stage 3b). Research can move a score up or down by up to 20 points. A COOL lead that research confirms is a strong operator will move to WARM. A lead that turns out to have overstated their scale will drop accordingly.

Section 06 — Reliability

What happens when something goes wrong

The pipeline is designed around one principle: an imperfect notification delivered is always better than a perfect report that never arrives. No failure at any stage — database error, search tool unavailable, unexpected crash — is allowed to silently stop the pipeline.

Database failures

Never stop the pipeline

If a database write fails, the data stays in memory and the pipeline moves forward. The Telegram notification is sent regardless. The team always gets the report.

Web search failures

Handled per query

If a search tool fails, a fallback tool is tried once. If both fail, that field is marked "Not found" and the next query runs. Research that returns nothing is still reported honestly — the team knows the company has low web presence.

Unexpected errors

Global catch-all

If anything crashes mid-pipeline, whatever data was collected at that point is packaged and sent to Telegram immediately. The team is always notified — even on a partial run.

Duplicate entries

Caught before insert

The duplicate check always runs before any new record is created, using email address as the unique identifier. If the email exists, the original record is updated and the team receives both the new submission and the original report side by side.
