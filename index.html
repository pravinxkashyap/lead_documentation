Section 01 — Overview

What this system does

MSS runs three separate brands — Trakop, DairyTech.ai, and Master Software Solutions — each with its own contact form, its own ideal customer profile, and its own sales pitch. When a prospect submits any of these forms, someone on the team has to act on it: understand who this person is, whether the company is a real fit, and how to approach the first conversation. That work takes time, effort, and skill — and it has to happen for every single inbound lead, across three brands, every day.

This system is built to take the preparation off that person's plate — not the judgment. Before anyone on the team even opens a lead, the system has already validated the submission, identified the brand, researched the company across the web with up to seven targeted searches, and scored the fit against a 100-point Ideal Customer Profile. By the time the team sees the Telegram notification, the groundwork is done. They can focus entirely on the conversation — not on the lookup.

The intent This system is an augmentation tool, not a replacement. Every lead decision — who to call, how to pitch, whether to follow up — stays with the team. The system handles the research, scoring, and preparation so that those decisions can be made faster and with better information.
Fig 1.1 — End-to-end flow across all three brands

Who interacts with this system

Prospects

Fill the enquiry form

Visit any of the three brand forms, fill in their details, and submit. They see a confirmation. The pipeline runs entirely silently in the background — prospects never know it exists.

Sales team

Make better decisions, faster

Receives a fully researched briefing on Telegram for every lead — including ICP score, company background, key contacts, and a personalised pitch angle. The research is already done. The team decides what to do with it.

Section 04 — Source Detection

How the system knows which brand a lead came from

Three different forms feed into one pipeline. Before any validation or scoring runs, the system must identify which brand a lead came from — because the ICP scoring model, web search queries, and sales pitch are all different for each brand. This identification step is called Source Detection, and it runs before everything else.

Important — no explicit source field Currently, none of the three forms include a hidden source field in their HTML. Source detection works entirely by inspecting which form fields are present in the submitted data. If all three forms ever converge on identical field names, detection could become unreliable — adding an explicit hidden field to each form is the long-term fix.

Section 05 — Pipeline Stages

The five-stage pipeline — every step explained

Once the source is detected and fields are normalised, the pipeline runs through five stages in strict order. Each stage either advances the lead or stops it and notifies the sales team. The pipeline always sends a Telegram notification — no lead is ever silently dropped.

1

Stage 1 — VALIDATE

Four sequential checks — all run before anything is saved to the database. The database insert happens only if all four checks pass.

  • 1.1 Missing data — Checks required fields. Flags as incomplete if any are missing or email is invalid. For MSS, business_type is inferred from the message rather than required on the form.
  • 1.2 Spam check — Looks for gibberish names, disposable email domains (mailinator, yopmail, etc.), requirements under 5 meaningful words, or obviously fake phone numbers.
  • 1.3 Duplicate check — Queries the database by email address only. Company name is intentionally ignored (same company can have different names; different companies can share a name). If the email already exists, retrieves the original lead's full record and includes it in the Telegram notification.
  • 1.4 Competitor check — Checks the email domain against a hardcoded competitor list (Tookan, OnFleet, Routific, Bringg, etc.) and a live competitor table in the database.
If any check fails No DB insert. Telegram notification sent immediately. Pipeline stops. The only exception: for duplicates, the original record is updated to mark it as a duplicate target — no new record is created.
2

Stage 2 — ICP SCORING (Preliminary)

The lead is scored against the Ideal Customer Profile for the detected brand. No web searches — this is a form-data-only score. Scored in 5 dimensions, totalled (max 110), then normalised to a 0–100 scale.

  • trakop source → Trakop ICP (delivery model fit, operational volume, pain points, decision maker, geography)
  • dairytech source → DairyTech.ai ICP (dairy domain fit, dairy scale, dairy pain points, decision maker, geography)
  • mss source → MSS ICP (software project need, business scale/budget, pain clarity, decision maker, geography)
  • Score < 30 (COLD) → Send cold rejection Telegram → STOP
  • Score 30–49 (COOL) → Proceed to Stage 3 — never reject COOL without research
  • Score 50+ (WARM/HOT) → Proceed to Stage 3
3

Stage 3 — WEB RESEARCH (Mandatory)

Up to 7 targeted web searches are run to gather real, cited intelligence on the company. All findings must come from actual search results — no training knowledge. Results must come from at least 3 different source domains.

  • Queries 1–2: Company overview and country profile (always run)
  • Queries 3–4: LinkedIn contacts, recent news (run if contacts/size unclear)
  • Queries 5–6: ZoomInfo/Crunchbase, business-specific operational terms
  • Query 7: Dedicated revenue search — {company} revenue OR turnover OR valuation OR funding (always run)
  • Low web presence: handled gracefully. Try alternate queries (founder name, broader company name, local directories). Low presence ≠ bad lead — especially in WhatsApp-heavy markets.
  • B2C / individual leads: search uses the person's name instead of company. Look for Facebook pages, Google Maps, WhatsApp Business listings.
3b

Stage 3b — VALIDATION (Research Cross-Check)

The preliminary ICP score is revisited using what was actually found online. This stage always runs — even if Stage 3 found nothing.

  • Business type check — Does research confirm what the form said? Flag any mismatch.
  • Scale check — Does research confirm stated order volume / customer count? Adjust company size and score if significantly different.
  • Competitor re-check — Did any research finding reveal this is a delivery software vendor? If yes → reject as competitor.
  • ICP score revision — Adjust score ±5 to ±20 points based on findings. No research = 0 change.
  • Discrepancy flagging — Any clear contradiction between form data and research is documented explicitly.
  • Revised score < 30 → Post-research cold rejection (includes all research findings in Telegram) → STOP
  • Competitor revealed → Send competitor flag Telegram → STOP
  • Otherwise → proceed to Stage 4
4

Stage 4 — DELIVER

The full lead report is compiled and sent to the sales team on Telegram. The DB is updated to status = notified. This stage always runs for leads that reach it — DB failures never block delivery.

  • ICP score and full dimensional breakdown
  • Company research (8–12 bullets with source URLs, blank line between each)
  • Validation findings (separate section from research)
  • Revenue estimate — direct, proxy-calculated, or "Not found" only if truly no data
  • Sales enablement: brand-specific icebreaker, pain point, pitch strategy
Company name inference If the form has no company name and the email is a business domain (not Gmail/Yahoo), the system infers the company name from the email domain — e.g. info@freshmill.co.ke → Fresh Mill. If the email is personal and no company is provided, company_name is set to "Individual" and the lead is treated as a B2C operator.
Section 06 — ICP Scoring

How leads are scored — three models, one formula

Every lead is scored on five dimensions. The same structure applies to all three brands — but the criteria inside each dimension differ based on what matters for that brand. Raw scores total up to 110 points maximum, then are normalised to a clean 0–100 scale by dividing by 1.10 and rounding.

Normalisation formula Total raw points (A+B+C+D+E) ÷ 1.10 = ICP score (0–100). This preserves relative differences between leads while fitting everything into a clean 100-point scale.

The four tiers

80–100 HOT Strong fit, clear need. Prioritise immediately.
50–79 WARM Decent fit, some gaps. Worth reaching out.
30–49 COOL Weak signals. Proceed to research before deciding.
0–29 COLD Poor fit. Reject without research.
COOL leads are never rejected without research A COOL score (30–49) means the form data alone is insufficient to decide. The pipeline always runs Stage 3 web research for COOL leads. The research may lift the score into WARM or confirm it should be rejected.

Five dimensions — all three models

DimensionMax ptsTrakop asksDairyTech.ai asksMSS asks
A. Business / Domain Fit 30 Does this business run recurring scheduled deliveries with its own fleet? Does this business operate in the dairy supply chain? Does this business need custom software, ERP/CRM, or AI?
B. Operational Scale 25 Orders/day and customers in Trakop sweet spot (20–1,000/day)? Dairy volume — liters/day, routes, SKUs, plant count? Business size and budget signal — employee count, project scope?
C. Pain Points 25 Manual routes, WhatsApp orders, subscription chaos, container tracking? Inventory spoilage, farmer payment disputes, batch traceability, cold chain? Specific problem described — replacing spreadsheets, need to automate X?
D. Decision Maker 20 Same across all brands: Owner/CEO (18–20) → Ops Manager (13–17) → Mid-level (7–12) → Generic (4–6) → Shared inbox (0)
E. Geography 10 Same across all brands: India/GCC (8–10) → SE Asia/Africa/LATAM (5–7) → USA (3–4) → Western Europe (1–2) → Unknown (0)

The Stage 2 score is preliminary — based only on form data. After web research (Stage 3), the score is revised up or down based on findings:

Section 07 — Web Research

How the system researches every company

Stage 3 is mandatory — it runs for every lead that is not COLD or stopped earlier. The AI agent runs up to 7 targeted searches, reads the top 7 results per query, and extracts only data that is directly stated in actual search results. Training knowledge is banned — every claim must be cited with a source URL.

Search tool waterfall

Primary

SearXNG plugin

Used for queries 1–4. Aggregates results from multiple engines. No API key required.

Fallback

DDG web search

Used if SearXNG fails for a query. Primary tool for queries 5–6.

Final fallback

web-search / local-web-search

Only used if both primary tools fail for the same query. One attempt maximum per query.

Why a dedicated revenue query (Q7)? Revenue data rarely surfaces in general company searches. The dedicated Q7 query targets it directly — looking for press mentions of ARR, D&B estimates, Crunchbase funding, or any reported turnover figure. If nothing is found after 7 queries, only then is "Not found" acceptable.

Revenue estimation rules

  • 1

    Direct figure

    Revenue stated in a news article, Crunchbase, D&B, or company profile → use verbatim with citation. E.g. "~$2M ARR (crunchbase.com)"

  • 2

    Proxy calculation

    No direct figure but customer count + visible pricing are known → calculate and label clearly. E.g. "Est. $432K/yr (800 customers × $45/mo avg — from website pricing)"

  • 3

    "Not found"

    Only acceptable after all 7 queries return zero revenue signals. Not a first-resort answer.

    • Low web presence

    Some clients — particularly small operators or businesses in markets with low internet penetration — have minimal online presence. This is expected and common. The pipeline handles it:

    • Try the founder's name as a search anchor if company searches fail
    • Try a simpler version of the company name (drop "Ltd", "Group", "Pvt")
    • Try local directory or registry searches for the city
    • Flag "low web presence" explicitly in the Telegram report for the team's awareness
    • Low web presence alone does NOT lower the ICP score — score on form data
    • In WhatsApp-heavy markets (Africa, South Asia, Middle East) this is especially normal
    Section 08 — Database

    What gets stored and how

    All lead data is stored in a single SQLite file at db/leads.db. The AI agent never writes to it directly — it calls db.js from the command line, which runs the appropriate SQL query and returns results as JSON. Three tables exist.

    How db.js works

    The agent runs shell commands like:

    ~/.nvm/versions/node/v22.22.2/bin/node lead-automation/scripts/db.js insert '{"first_name":"Rajesh","email":"r@acme.com",...}'

    The full Node.js binary path is required — bare node is never used. Supported commands: insert, get, update, duplicate, competitors, add-competitor, log, stats, recent, status.

    Critical: check before insert The duplicate check (db.js duplicate) always runs before the lead is inserted. Inserting first and then checking would cause a false duplicate match against the just-inserted record. The insert only happens after all Stage 1 checks pass.

    Duplicate notification — what it looks like

    When the pipeline detects a duplicate email, it fetches the original lead's full record from the database and includes it in the notification. The team can see both the new submission and the complete original report side by side.

    Why this matters A duplicate submission could mean the prospect re-submitted, someone from the same company submitted again, or a sales handoff failed and the lead is circling back. Including the original report means the team can immediately see the history — was the original lead HOT? Was it already contacted? What did the research find? — without needing to look anything up.
    Section 10 — Error Handling

    What happens when things go wrong

    The pipeline is built around one core principle: an imperfect result delivered is always better than a perfect result that never arrives. No failure at any stage — database errors, search tool failures, or even unexpected crashes — is allowed to silently stop the pipeline without a Telegram notification.

    Error hierarchy

    Failure typeWhat happensDoes pipeline stop?
    DB command failsLog the error. Keep all data in memory. Proceed to next step. Retry only if it was a JSON formatting error the agent made — once only.Never
    DB insert failsSet lead_id = null. Skip all subsequent DB writes that need an ID. Still proceed to Stage 2 and send Telegram at Stage 4.Never
    Single web search failsOne attempt on primary tool → one attempt on fallback → if both fail → mark "Not found" → next query. Never try a third tool for the same query.Never
    All web searches failLog "Web search unavailable". Set all research fields to "Not found". Proceed immediately to Stage 3b with form data only.Never
    Stage crashes / unhandled errorGlobal catch-all: collect whatever data exists in memory at the point of failure. Send a Telegram notification with all available fields — use "Error — not retrieved" for anything that could not be populated.Telegram always sent
    Loop detection Before every DB command or web search, the agent checks: "Have I run this exact action before in this pipeline run?" If yes → mark it failed → advance immediately. This prevents the agent from getting stuck in a retry loop on a command it has already attempted. Same action twice = failed action, no exceptions.

    Shell command JSON safety

    All database commands pass values inside a JSON string wrapped in single quotes. This means certain characters must be sanitised before building the shell command. The agent does this before every DB write:

    CharacterReplace withWhy
    ' (apostrophe)space or omitWould break the outer single-quote shell wrapping
    " (double quote)space or omitWould break the JSON string values
    ` (backtick)removeShell command substitution
    \n \r (newlines)spaceBreaks multi-line shell arguments

    Section 11 — Absolute Rules

    The 19 non-negotiable rules

    These rules are embedded in LEAD_PIPELINE.md and override everything else. The AI agent is instructed to treat these as hard constraints — no exceptions, no edge cases.

    1. Duplicate check runs BEFORE insert. Never insert first and check after. This is fixed and non-negotiable.
    2. Always send Telegram. Every lead gets a notification — flagged, rejected, or full report.
    3. Never invent data. If you cannot cite a search result for a claim — use "Not found."
    4. Training data is banned in Stages 3 and 3b. Every company-specific fact must come from a web search result returned in this session.
    5. DB failures never stop the pipeline. Log the error, keep data in memory, move forward.
    6. Web search failures never stop the pipeline. Log failed queries, fill affected fields as "Not found", move forward.
    7. Never loop. Same action twice = failed action. Mark it and advance immediately.
    8. Stage 3b always runs. Even if Stage 3 found nothing. Even if all searches failed.
    9. Stage 4 always runs unless the pipeline stopped at Stage 1 or Stage 2 cold rejection. Even on unexpected crashes — the global catch-all applies: send a Telegram with whatever data is available.
    10. Never read local source files. Do not inspect db.js or any other script. Only run the documented CLI commands.
    11. Sales Enablement must be specific. Generic filler is a failure. Reference actual research findings or specific form data.
    12. ICP tier values are always uppercase: HOT, WARM, COOL, COLD — never mixed case.
    13. COOL leads (30–49) always proceed to Stage 3. Never reject COOL without research confirmation.
    14. lead_id is always the integer from the insert output. Never guess it or use a placeholder in a DB command — skip the command instead if the ID is unknown.
    15. Sanitise & before every shell command. Replace with and in all field values.
    16. Integer values in JSON must be bare numbers, not quoted strings. "icp_score":78 not "icp_score":"78".
    17. Source detection runs first, before Stage 1. Always identify and store the source (trakop, dairytech, mss). The source drives ICP scoring, research queries, solution_product, and pitch strategy.
    18. Post-research rejections use the Post-Research Cold Rejection Telegram format. If a lead is rejected in Stage 3b, include all research findings and validation bullets — not just the ICP score and reason.
    19. ICP scoring must match the detected source. Do not score a DairyTech.ai lead with Trakop ICP criteria or vice versa.
